“You gettee in,” he added, motioning to me with his tomahawk, and
throwing the clothes to one side. He really did this in not only a civil
but a really kind and charitable way. I stood looking at him a mo-
ment. For all his tattooings he was on the whole a clean, comely look-
ing cannibal. ‘“What’s all this fuss I have been making about,”
thought I to myself—“the man’s a human being just as I am: he has
just as much reason to fear me as I have to be afraid of him. Better
sleep with a sober cannibal than a drunken Christian.”

“Landlord,” said I, “tell him to stash his tomahawk there, or pipe,
or whatever you call it; tell him to stop smoking, in short, and I will
turn in with him. But I don’t fancy having a man smoking in bed
with me. It’s dangerous. Besides, I ain’t insured.”

This being told to Queequeg, he at once complied, and again politely
motioned me to get into bed—rolling over to one side as much as to
say—“I won’t touch a leg of ye.”

“Good-night, landlord,” said I; “you may go.”

I turned in, and never slept better in my life.
